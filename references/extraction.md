# Extracting ingredients and operations

## Ingredients

- Take the ingredient list as the authority for quantities; the method text often omits them.
- Where the method mentions an ingredient absent from the list (salt, water, oil for the pan), add a row with the quantity implied by the method, or "to taste".
- Divided ingredients ("2 tablespoons oil, divided") become one row per use. Read the method to find the split; if it is not stated, split evenly and note the assumption in the follow-up sentences.
- Sub-recipe references ("1 recipe pie dough") become a single row unless the user also wants the sub-recipe expanded; if expanding, it becomes its own component group.
- Keep headnote preferences (recommended brands, substitutions) out of the table; mention them after it if useful.

## Operations

Recipe steps are paragraphs that bundle several operations. Split them at every point where either the set of ingredients changes or a time/temperature boundary occurs. The sentence "Heat oil until shimmering. Add garlic, salt, oregano, and pepper flakes; cook until fragrant, about 30 seconds. Stir in tomatoes and sugar; bring to simmer, reduce heat, simmer until thickened, about 20 minutes." contains three operations:

1. heat (oil) — until shimmering
2. cook 30 s (oil + garlic + salt + oregano + flakes)
3. simmer 20 min (previous + tomatoes + sugar)

Operations that change no ingredient set but have their own timing (rest, chill, cool, flip) still count; they become cells spanning the same rows as the cell to their left.

Discard operations that are purely logistical and untimed ("transfer to a plate", "set aside") unless the vessel change matters to the cook later. "Cover and keep warm" is worth keeping because it tells the cook the sauce is parked.

## Parallelism

The table cannot show that two operations run concurrently. Note this in the follow-up text: which long passive steps (simmer, rest, chill, rise, preheat) overlap, and what to start first.

## Conversions

Standard figures for the metric parentheses:

| ingredient | 1 cup |
|---|---|
| all-purpose flour | 120–125 g |
| granulated sugar | 200 g |
| brown sugar, packed | 220 g |
| butter | 227 g (1 stick = 113 g = 4 oz) |
| cocoa powder | 85–90 g |
| panko | 60 g (½ cup = 30 g) |
| grated hard cheese | 90–100 g loosely |
| shredded soft cheese | 110–115 g |
| water, milk, oil | 240 mL |

1 Tbs = 15 mL; 1 tsp = 5 mL; kosher salt 1 tsp ≈ 5–6 g (Diamond Crystal is about half the density of Morton; use ~3 g if the source specifies Diamond). Oven temperatures: 350°F = 175°C, 375°F = 190°C, 400°F = 200°C, 425°F = 220°C, 450°F = 230°C.
